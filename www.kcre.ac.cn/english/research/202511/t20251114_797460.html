Glacial lakes in Third Pole region impacted by climate change: report

2015-12-25

Chinese scientists have found that glacial lakes in the Third Pole region of the Earth are experiencing an increase in size due to global warming. The study was the first comprehensive study of the region to be conducted for 1990, 2000, and 2010.

The region encompassing Pamir, the Hindu Kush, Himalayan mountains and the Tibetan Plateau is widely known as the Third Pole. The region's ice fields contain the largest reserve of fresh water outside the polar regions.

Looking at the Tibetan Plateau, it is the largest and highest plateau on Earth and is the source of 10 major river systems, supplying irrigation, power and drinking water to over 1.3 billion people, almost 20 percent of the world's population.

Using Landsat data to form a comprehensive inventory

The importance of this Third Pole region cannot be dismissed, especially with global warming, and this is one of the reasons the inventory and study of the glacial lakes in the region was undertaken by the Institute of Tibetan Plateau Research, under the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Additionally, accurate information was lacking on the distribution, actual numbers of lakes, and changes in the glacial lakes that might have occurred due to global warming. To get the data needed for the inventory, Landsat TM/ETM photos plus the data was used. Glacial lake spatial distributions, corresponding areas, and temporal distribution changes were then studied and calculated.

Results of the study were surprising

In 1990, there were 4,602 glacial lakes, covering a total area of 553.9 ± 90km2. In 2000, there were 4,981 lakes, covering a total area of 581.2 ± 97km2. And in 2010, there were 5,701 glacial lakes, covering a total area of 682.4 ± 110 km2.

The lakes were primarily located in the Brahmaputra (39 percent), Indus (28 percent), and Amu Darya (10 percent) basins. The researchers described three interesting changes in glacial lakes:

(1.) Small lakes (< 0.2 km2), are much more sensitive to climate change.

(2.) Lakes that are closer to glaciers, and at higher altitudes, particularly the lakes connected to the termini of glaciers have undergone large changes in area.

(3.) Glacier-fed lakes are dominant in both quantity and overall area. They also are showing a much faster expansion rate than non-glacier fed lakes.

The study gives scientists an important database for future studies on global warming's impact on Third Pole glacial lakes and the corresponding trends in negative glacier mass balance patterns. More importantly, it gives scientists a basis for long-term monitoring of outburst flood disasters caused by glacial lakes in the Third Pole.

This most interesting study was published in the journal Global and Planetary Change on June 2, 2015, under the title: "An inventory of glacial lakes in the Third Pole region and their changes in response to global warming". (Digital Journal)
